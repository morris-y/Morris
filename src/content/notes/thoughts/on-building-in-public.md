# On Building in Public

Building in public is a forcing function for clarity. When you have to explain what you built and why, you can't hide behind jargon or slide-deck abstraction.

## What I've Learned

The hardest part isn't the vulnerability—it's the **precision**. Writing "I shipped a feature" is easy. Writing what the feature actually does, who it's for, and whether it worked? That requires honest reflection.

Prediction markets taught me this most clearly. Every position is a public statement. You're not just saying "I think X will happen"—you're *betting on it*, and the market sees your conviction level in real time.

## The Threshold Problem

Not everything is worth sharing publicly. The question I ask:

> Does putting this out there create a feedback loop that makes the thing better?

If yes → publish. If it's just noise → keep it as a note.

This post, for example, is somewhere in between. Writing it anyway.
