# Prediction Markets as Information Systems

Most people look at prediction markets and see gambling. I see a **distributed information aggregation protocol**.

## The Core Insight

Markets are efficient at something specific: incorporating dispersed, heterogeneous information into a single number. A prediction market about "Will X happen by Y date" is asking millions of people to encode everything they know about X into a probability.

That's not gambling—that's an information system with a built-in incentive to be honest.

## Why This Matters for Products

At Hubble, we built tools on top of Polymarket's liquidity. The challenge wasn't the trading mechanics—it was helping users understand what the market was *actually saying*.

A 73% probability isn't just a number. It's:
- The weighted average of many people's models
- A real-time update to prior beliefs
- A signal that something specific is priced in

Building UI for this required rethinking information hierarchy entirely.

## What I Still Don't Know

- How much of prediction market "accuracy" is genuine information aggregation vs self-fulfilling dynamics?
- Does the act of building better trading tools change market behavior in ways that reduce the signal?

These questions keep me up at night. In a good way.
