# Shipping Fast vs Shipping Right

This is a false dichotomy that keeps showing up in PM conversations. Let me try to dissolve it.

## The Real Question

The question isn't "fast or right"—it's **"what does 'right' mean at this stage?"**

In month 1 of a product, "right" means working well enough to learn from. In month 18, "right" means production-grade reliability. The standard changes; the commitment to shipping something real doesn't.

## A Framework I Use

For any given feature, I ask:

1. **What's the riskiest assumption?** (Usually: does anyone care?)
2. **What's the cheapest test of that assumption?**
3. **What's the minimum implementation that runs that test?**

That third question is your actual definition of "right" right now.

## The Binance Version of This

At Binance, we shipped 27 features in 6 months. That sounds fast. But most of them were small, well-scoped, and went through proper QA. "Fast" was the output of clarity, not the cause.

The features that took forever were the ones where we didn't agree on what "done" meant.

## The Hubble Version

At Hubble AI, we shipped the prediction markets terminal to $200k monthly volume in 2 months. Two people. The speed came from ruthless prioritization: we built exactly what traders needed to make decisions, nothing else.

Constraint clarifies.
