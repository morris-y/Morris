## 3. Hidden Failure Mode in the Build Strategy

The common strategy of "build something impressive, then show founders" has a hidden failure mode.

Most builders create something that makes founders think:

> "This is good, but we are already thinking about this."

This happens because the builder solves an obvious problem. Obvious problems can be inferred from product roadmaps, public interviews, Twitter discussions, job descriptions, and visible product gaps.

That kind of build may demonstrate skill, but it does not create strategic surprise.

The stronger build solves a problem where:

- The founder already knows the issue is painful.
- The founder has not publicly explained how they plan to solve it.
- The issue is closer to business survival than incremental improvement.

The goal is to move from:

> "This would improve the product."

to:

> "This addresses something that could seriously hurt the company if unresolved."

## 4. Research Question That Changes the Frame

Instead of researching:

> What is this company building?

Ask:

> If this company fails in six months, what is the most likely reason?

This question changes the research direction from improvement to survival.

Founders are often numb to generic improvement suggestions. They are much more sensitive to existential risks, hidden bottlenecks, and threats to the company's core thesis.

Example research angles:

- Distribution failure: users like the product but do not form a durable habit.
- Liquidity failure: markets exist but do not attract enough trading depth.
- Trust failure: users do not trust prices, data freshness, settlement, or counterparty risk.
- Creator failure: builders or market creators do not have enough incentive to keep producing.
- Retention failure: users come for events but do not return during normal cycles.
- Infrastructure failure: latency, indexing, data correctness, or uptime limits high-value use cases.
- Regulatory/compliance failure: expansion is slowed by unclear jurisdiction, risk controls, or institutional constraints.
- Monetization failure: usage grows but revenue capture remains weak or conflicts with user incentives.
